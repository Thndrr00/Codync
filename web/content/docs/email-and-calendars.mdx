These two accounts live on your computer, not in the cloud. Your bots can read and use them, and the
apps only show what the computer returns.

## Email

Open **Settings › Email** on the desktop, or **Workspace › Email** on the iPhone and Android app, and
add an account:

- **Name** and **Address**: what the account is called and the address it sends from.
- **Password**: your mail password or, where the provider requires one, an app password. It goes into
  this computer's encrypted vault and is never shown again.
- **IMAP server** and **SMTP server**: for example `imap.example.com` and `smtp.example.com`. IMAP uses
  TLS on port 993; SMTP uses STARTTLS on 587.

**Read inbox** shows the newest headers: sender, subject and date. **Send an email** sends a short
plain-text message from the selected account. Sending is real and immediate; the bot asks first when a
task needs it, unless you told it to send.

The computer needs Python 3 (`python3`, `python` or `py` on `PATH`) for the small mail bridge. No mail
account is created by Codync and nothing is stored outside the computer.

Bots get two tools: `email_inbox` (read-only headers) and `email_send` (a real send).

## Calendars

Open **Settings › Calendars** on the desktop, or **Workspace › Calendar** on a phone, and paste the
read-only `.ics` address of a calendar: Google, Apple, Outlook or any calendar that offers an export.
Up to twenty feeds are kept. The computer refreshes a feed at most every ten minutes and shows the next
two weeks, including all-day events.

Calendars are read-only: Codync never edits or invites anyone. Bots get `calendar_agenda` to answer
"what's on my calendar this week", and the daily digest includes today's events.

## News feeds

**Settings › Feeds** on the desktop, or the same section under **Workspace › Calendar** on a phone,
follows RSS and Atom feeds: blogs, news sites, podcasts. Paste the feed address and a name. Codync reads
it at most every ten minutes, keeps the newest headlines and never writes to the site. **Only this**
filters the list to one feed; **Remove** forgets it.

Bots read the same items with the read-only `news_feed` tool and can open a link with `clip_page`.

## Removing an account

**Remove account** forgets the server settings and deletes the password from the vault. The mail server
itself is untouched.
