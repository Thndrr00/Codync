Talk to your bots from WhatsApp, Telegram, Slack, Discord or Matrix. Only the people you allow can write to a bot there, and the bot answers with its own agent, folder and permissions.

## Connect a channel

Channels belong to a computer and are set up on it. Open **Extensions → Channels** on the desktop or iPhone app, pick the channel and use its page:

- **WhatsApp:** link a number with the QR code, choose the bot that answers it, and let chats or people in. A separate number is safer: WhatsApp can block one it sees as automated.
- **Telegram:** add the bot @BotFather gave you, choose the Codync bot it answers for, then send `/start` with its pairing code from Telegram.
- **Slack:** connect the workspace's app with its bot token, app token and signing secret; each channel is bound to a Codync bot and to the Slack users allowed in it.
- **Discord:** connect the server's bot token, then bind channel ids to Codync bots and list the people allowed in each.
- **Matrix:** sign in with the bot account's homeserver and an access token or password, then bind a room id or alias to a Codync bot and list the Matrix users allowed there. Invite the account to the rooms first; encrypted rooms are not decrypted yet.

The same controls remain in **Settings → General → Channels**, with a shortcut to Extensions. Each channel's page shows its state, the bot that answers, the people allowed, its memory and its picture.

Tokens stay in the computer's credential vault. A paired phone can see the channels, pair or remove someone, and change what a channel remembers.

## Voice notes

Allowed Telegram, Slack and Discord senders can send a voice note using the computer's
existing OpenAI or Gemini speech connection. The host adds its transcript to the bot's
message; audio-only messages work too. Slack needs the app's `files:read` scope. Audio
is limited to 20 MiB and sent to the configured speech provider, with its usual charges.
See [messaging voice notes](/docs/voice#messaging-voice-notes) for supported behavior
and failure notices. Removing a sender or disconnecting the channel revokes queued input.

## Signal, Email and Home Assistant

Install these native Hermes channels from **Extensions → Apps**, then enable the connection
for exactly one bot. The terminal offers the same setup in its connector catalog. Leave
optional account fields blank to reuse an existing Hermes profile and its credentials;
Home Assistant requires explicit watched entity IDs. Each connection has its own session.

Signal keeps the existing signal-cli account and group policy. Email uses the existing
IMAP/SMTP mailbox and requires authenticated senders. An optional signed RFC822 webhook
can receive mail through the host instead of polling alone. Home Assistant delivers watched
state changes as text and sends replies as native persistent notifications.

For a new Signal or Email sender, set a one-use DM pairing code in the app fields and send
`/pair <code>` within ten minutes after connection. This never bypasses Email sender
authentication. Native approval messages list the original choices; only the requesting
sender can answer with `/approve <code> <number>`. Files and quoted replies are retained,
and voice notes use the host's existing speech connection. Uncertain outbound sends are
kept as unknown rather than automatically duplicated.

## Channel memory

Each Telegram bot, Slack channel, Discord channel and Matrix room has a **Channel memory**: what the bot should know about that conversation, such as the project it covers or how its people like answers. It is given to the bot before every message from that channel and saved on its own, so it survives a reconnect.

A channel also wears a **Channel picture**: one of twenty icons or, the first choice, the faces of its members. It shows beside the channel in Settings.

Keep it short and stable. Anything that changes per message belongs in the message; anything that belongs to every chat belongs in the bot's [memory](/docs/memory).

## Linked conversations

A channel conversation can share a Codync thread, so the app, a voice call and Slack (or Telegram or Discord) keep one context. A Slack thread or Discord channel gets a durable root of its own from its first message; linking moves later messages and replies to the thread you choose. Linking does not claim to move earlier messages: they stay in the old conversation. The computer exposes this as `conversationLinks`, `linkConversation` and `unlinkConversation`; a call started from a thread carries that thread with `startCall`'s `threadId`. A channel's settings on the desktop and the phones list the links it has, remove one, and link the channel to a thread you pick; for Slack and Telegram you enter the conversation's own id (a thread timestamp or a chat id), because that is how the computer names it.

See [one conversation across text, calls and channels](/docs/conversations#one-conversation-across-text-calls-and-channels).

## Approvals and routines

An [approval card](/docs/conversations#approval-cards) arrives in the channel with a button per option; anyone allowed there may answer, and the first answer wins. Slack shows buttons on the card, Discord and Telegram show them under it, WhatsApp lists the options as numbered replies, and Matrix shows a numbered card answered by a reply or a reaction. Mentioning the bot in a Slack or Telegram channel can also start a [routine](/docs/routines); those triggers are edited on the computer.
